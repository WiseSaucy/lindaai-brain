# Sea Moss on Facebook/Instagram: Ads Compliance Playbook

*Last updated: August 2026. Meta changes these rules often — recheck the
[Health and Wellness ad standard](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/)
and [Meta's Health and Wellness advertiser help page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2489235377779939)
before big campaign launches.*

Sea moss sits in Meta's "health and wellness" bucket (dietary/herbal supplement).
That category has the highest ad-rejection rates on the platform, and repeated
rejections are what actually kill ad accounts. This playbook covers what gets sea
moss sellers in trouble, what to say instead, and the account-hygiene rules that
keep you running long-term.

---

## 1. The two kinds of "trouble"

1. **Meta trouble** — ad rejections, restricted delivery, disabled ad account,
   disabled Business Manager. Caused by claim violations, landing-page
   violations, "circumventing systems" behavior, and bad customer feedback
   scores.
2. **Regulator trouble** — FDA warning letters and FTC actions. Sea moss brands
   have received FDA warning letters for disease claims (immunity/virus cures,
   thyroid treatment). The same claims that trip Meta also create legal risk, so
   fixing your claims solves both at once.

---

## 2. What changed recently (important)

- **July 22, 2026 — claims-based review.** Meta moved health/wellness
  enforcement from *product-based* to *claims-based*. Ads are no longer
  auto-rejected just for being a supplement or showing side-by-side imagery;
  they're rejected for **violating claims**. Supplements can generally run
  without 18+ age targeting **unless the ad makes weight-loss or weight-gain
  claims**. Still flat-out banned: disease-cure claims, blood-sugar/diabetes
  claims, "pinched fat" style imagery, statements of inferiority ("tired of
  being fat?"), sensational health claims, skin whitening, and
  sexual-enhancement positioning.
- **January 2025 — tracking restrictions.** Meta auto-categorizes domains/pixels
  as "health & wellness" (it crawls your website and reads your event data). A
  categorized data source loses lower-funnel events — **Purchase and Add to
  Cart can't be used for optimization or retargeting**. See §7.

## 3. Claims that get sea moss ads rejected (or accounts banned)

Never say these in ad copy, creative text overlays, headlines, videos, **or on
the landing page** (Meta scans the page too):

| Category | Examples to avoid |
|---|---|
| Disease treatment/cure/prevention | "heals thyroid issues", "fights viruses", "prevents colds", "cures inflammation", "lowers blood sugar", "helps with diabetes", "beats acid reflux", "anti-cancer" |
| Blood sugar / diabetes | Any mention tied to the product — explicitly banned category |
| Sexual enhancement | "great for the bedroom", "boosts libido/stamina", "nature's viagra" — sea moss is often hyped this way; this positioning is **banned outright** |
| Weight loss promises | "melt fat", "lose 10 lbs", "suppresses appetite" — triggers weight-loss rules + 18+ targeting; unrealistic outcomes are banned |
| Negative self-perception | "Tired of feeling fat/sick/tired?", "Fix your gut", pinched-fat imagery, mocking body types |
| Implying you know the viewer's condition | "Do YOU have thyroid problems?", "For people struggling with fatigue" — personal-attributes violation (second person + condition) |
| Medical authority claims | "Doctors are hiding this", "clinically proven" (without real substantiation), fake news-article formats |
| The "102 minerals" myth | "Contains 92/102 of the minerals your body needs" is scientifically unsupported → misleading-claims risk with Meta *and* FTC. Name real nutrients instead. |
| Detox / cleanse framing | "flushes toxins", "heavy metal detox" — treated as misleading health claims |

**Rule of thumb:** if the sentence would make sea moss sound like a drug
(treats, cures, prevents, fixes a named condition or symptom), cut it.

## 4. What you CAN say

Structure/function and lifestyle language, aimed at general wellness — not
conditions, not the viewer's body:

- **General wellness:** "supports your daily wellness routine", "a nutrient-rich
  addition to your mornings"
- **Real nutrition facts, stated plainly:** "naturally rich in iodine, zinc,
  magnesium, and potassium" (factual, no outcome promised)
- **Energy/lifestyle (soft):** "supports energy levels as part of your routine",
  "beauty from within", "supports skin and hair from the inside" — keep it
  support-language, never treatment language
- **Origin & quality:** "wildcrafted in St. Lucia", "small-batch", "no
  additives, no gels from powder", "lab-tested for purity"
- **Ritual & taste:** recipes, smoothie content, how-to-use videos, texture
  shots, unboxings
- **Brand story:** founder story, sourcing trips, community — this content
  almost never gets rejected and builds the account's trust history
- **Honest social proof:** real reviews about taste, quality, service, and
  routine — **screen out** customer quotes that make medical claims ("cleared my
  eczema") before using them in ads; a testimonial making a claim you can't
  make yourself is still a violation (Meta and FTC both)

**Word swaps:**

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "Boosts your immune system" | "Supports your wellness routine" |
| "Cures/helps thyroid" | (drop it entirely — name iodine as a nutrient, promise nothing) |
| "Detox your body" | "Clean, simple ingredients" |
| "Lose weight fast" | (drop it — or accept 18+ targeting and strict review) |
| "Do you struggle with low energy?" | "Meet the morning ritual people won't skip" |
| "Anti-inflammatory superfood" | "Mineral-rich superfood" |

## 6. Landing page + store compliance (Meta reads your website)

- **Match:** the landing page must sell what the ad shows, at the price shown,
  with no bait-and-switch. Never edit an ad's destination or claims *after*
  approval — that's "circumventing systems" (account-level ban territory).
- **Scrub the whole site** of disease claims — product pages, FAQs, blog posts,
  and customer reviews you feature. Since Jan 2025, Meta *crawls your domain* to
  categorize/restrict you, so condition claims anywhere on the site hurt even
  if the ad is clean.
- **Required trust pages:** contact info, privacy policy, terms, refund policy,
  realistic shipping times. (Your LindaAI site already models this pattern —
  do the same on the sea moss store.)
- **FDA disclaimer** on any page with structure/function claims: "These
  statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This
  product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."
- **Don't list sea moss in Facebook/Instagram Shops.** Meta's commerce policy
  prohibits ingestible supplements as shop listings — run ads to your own
  website instead. A rejected catalog can drag down the whole commerce account.
- **Influencer/UGC ads:** anyone with a material connection must disclose
  (#ad), and their claims count as your claims (FTC endorsement rules).

**This brand's specifics:**

- **Domain:** wiseseamoss.com (as of this writing, the domain isn't
  resolving — confirm DNS/hosting is live before starting Meta business
  verification or running ads; Meta checks the live site).
- **Platform: Shopify.** In Shopify admin, do **not** enable the Facebook &
  Instagram sales channel's product sync/catalog for the sea moss listings —
  that publishes to Facebook/Instagram Shops, which prohibits ingestible

  Pixel/Conversions API setup only — not catalog sync. Shopify's built-in

  expected for Meta Business Manager verification and general trust — set
  this up before verifying the business.

## 7. The tracking gotcha (why your Purchase optimization may break)

Since January 2025, data sources Meta categorizes as "health & wellness" are
blocked from sharing lower-funnel events — **Purchase and Add to Cart get
suppressed** even via the Conversions API, so you can't optimize or retarget on
them.

What to do:

1. **Check Events Manager** → your pixel/data source → look for a data-sharing
   restriction notice, and see which category Meta assigned.
2. **Request a review** if you're miscategorized — a clean, claims-free website
   materially improves the odds (another reason for §6).
3. If restricted: optimize to the highest still-allowed event (e.g. landing
   page views / a custom mid-funnel event), keep the Conversions API in place
   for the events that ARE allowed, and measure real sales with UTMs +
   post-purchase surveys + your store's own analytics instead of trusting Ads
   Manager numbers.

## 8. Account hygiene: how sellers actually get banned

Rejections don't kill accounts — *patterns* do. Health/wellness ads get rejected
at 10–15%+ vs the ~1–5% platform norm, and Meta reads repeated violations as
"risk to the platform."

- **Set up properly:** Business Manager (business.facebook.com), complete
  business verification, two-factor auth on every admin, real business name and
  domain everywhere.
- **Warm up:** start with 2–4 clean, soft ads (ritual/quality angles) at modest
  budget; let the account build history before testing edgier angles.
- **When an ad is rejected:** don't rapid-fire resubmit clones — that's the
  pattern that escalates to account review. Either fix the specific claim and
  resubmit once, or **appeal once through Account Quality**
  (business.facebook.com/accountquality) if you believe it's a false positive.
  Machine review produces false positives constantly; a calm appeal usually
  resolves them.
- **Never:** cloak or redirect traffic, edit approved ads to add claims, run
  claims in comments you couldn't run in the ad, or spin up new ad
  accounts/pages to dodge a restriction — circumvention bans are the permanent
  kind.
- **Customer Feedback Score:** Meta surveys your buyers. Below 2 = delivery
  penalty (your ads cost more and reach less); below 1 = account disabled. Ship
  on time, answer messages fast, honor refunds, and don't let ad promises
  outrun the product. For perishable gel, be upfront about shipping windows
  and cold-pack handling.
- **Moderate ad comments:** hide/delete comments making medical claims; don't
  make claims in your own replies either.
- **Keep a claims log:** one doc listing every claim you use + why it's
  compliant (and substantiation for anything factual). If you're ever
  restricted or audited, this is what gets you back fast.

## 9. Unit economics & ad spend targets

Product economics (Aug 2026 pricing):

| Product | Price | Cost to make+ship | Gross profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jar | $40 | ~$10 | ~$30 |
| Capsules (60ct bottle) | $25 | ~$2.50–7 | ~$18–22 |
| Jar + capsule bundle | ~$65 | ~$13–17 | ~$48–52 |

**Strategy: lead with the jar, upsell capsules.** Jar photographs/videos better
for ads (gel texture, smoothie pours) and has the bigger margin cushion against
Meta's typical $20–40 CPA in this category. Capsules alone are too thin to lead
with (~$20 profit ≈ typical CPA) — position them as the convenience add-on
("no prep, take it on the go") at checkout. A bundle order tolerates a
$30–35 CPA comfortably.

**Breakage / reshipping (glass jars can break or get mishandled in transit):**

- A reship is a **margin cost, not a new ad cost** — never re-attribute it to
  CPA/CAC; it comes out of gross profit.
- At a realistic 3–5% breakage rate, expected cost per jar order rises from
  $10 to ~$10.30–10.50, so profit drops slightly to ~$29.50–29.70 — barely
  moves break-even.
- Budget a **$1–2/jar breakage reserve** for a safer effective break-even CPA
  of **~$28** instead of the raw $30.
- **Policy:** replace damaged jars free, no return required, ship
  immediately — protects reviews and the Customer Feedback Score (§8), which
  directly affects Meta delivery/account health.
- Invest in sturdier packaging (padded mailer, jar-in-jar insert) — cheap
  packaging is a false savings once reship costs and lost trust are counted.
- Capsules don't break, so leaning on them as upsell/bundle also lowers your
  overall breakage exposure.

**CPA targets:**

- Jar-only: healthy < **$20** (2x ROAS), workable up to **$28–30**, kill/fix
  the ad above that.
- Bundle: comfortable up to **$30–35** given ~$50 profit.
- Capsules solo: not a lead product — margin too thin to advertise directly.

## 10. What we'll actually be doing on Facebook (the plan)

1. **Foundation:** verify the business in Business Manager, 2FA on every admin,
   install the Meta Pixel + Conversions API on the site, confirm Events Manager
   category (watch for the health & wellness tracking restriction, §7).
2. **Site prep:** dedicated product/landing pages for jar and bundle (not the
   homepage, not Facebook/Instagram Shops), trust pages, FDA disclaimer, claims
   scrubbed per §3/§6.
3. **Creative:** compliant ad copy using the safe language and templates in
   §4–§5 — jar-led, ritual/quality/nutrition-facts angles, capsule positioned
   as the upsell.
4. **Launch:** 1 campaign, 2–3 ads, Advantage+ placements (Facebook + Instagram
   feed/Stories/Reels), **$20–50/day**, 2–4 soft warm-up ads before testing
   anything edgier (§8).
5. **Measure:** track against the CPA targets above using UTMs + store
   analytics (not just Ads Manager, in case Purchase events are suppressed).
6. **Iterate safely:** on a rejection, fix-and-resubmit once or appeal once —
   never resubmit-spam; scale what's under target CPA, kill/rework what isn't;
   watch Customer Feedback Score and ship damaged jars fast, no questions
   asked.
